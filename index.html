01 · The wall

A garden hung on a building

Living walls cool streets, hold rainwater and give birds and insects somewhere to live. They also need constant care: irrigation, feeding, pest control and replacing plants that fail. Today that work is done by technicians on ropes and lifts, and when the care stops, the wall dies.

This project hands the care to drones. The facade is 14 bays of 1.5 m across and 10 levels of 3 m up. Sedum mats sit on the exposed top levels, sedge grasses in the middle, shade ferns near the street, and climbing ivy throughout.

What the wall does to itself

Drying

Sun and wind pull water from the soil, fastest on the top levels.

→ water

Hunger

Growing plants use up nutrients and stop fruiting.

→ fertilizer, mixed into watering

Pests

An infestation spreads to the next module if nobody comes.

→ treat

Decay

Root rot and old growth brown a module, and it dies if nobody comes.

→ revive

02 · Behaviour

How a drone decides

No drone sees the whole wall, and no central planner tells it what to do. Each one follows the same short loop, and the pattern of care across the facade emerges from that.

  1. Look aroundScan the modules within sensing reach, plus any module a person has flagged.
  2. Pick the most urgentDead and decaying modules first, then pests, dry soil and hungry soil. Closer is better. Skip anything another drone has claimed.
  3. Check the batteryOnly go if there is charge for the trip, the job and the flight home.
  4. Fly the railsTravel along the joints of the wall: across on the slab lines, up and down on the bay lines.
  5. Give wayHold or step aside for drones at work and lower-numbered drones. Drones never overlap.
  6. Work, then look againWater, feed, treat or revive, then scan from where you are. Go home to recharge and refill when low.

03 · Proposals

A language model proposes. The wall decides.

Press Suggest next move and the playground writes up the state of the wall for a language model: every drone's position, battery, water and fertilizer, the most urgent modules, and the rules. The model replies with up to three moves. The simulation checks each one before it runs, and refuses anything that cannot work.

The model proposes

Send a drone to a module, change the fleet, the water trigger or the sensing reach, flag a module, or wait.

→

The simulation checks

Battery for the round trip, tank and hopper levels, claims by other drones, soil and nutrient levels, dock capacity.

→

Accepted

The drone drops its job and flies the route, drawn in blue with the proposal number.

Refused

The failure is drawn where it happens: the route in red, an X where the battery runs out, the drones circled.

A proposal

{"moves": [
  {"type": "dispatch", "drone": "D1",
   "module": "L7·B12", "task": "water",
   "why": "driest module near the top"},
  {"type": "dispatch", "drone": "D2",
   "module": "L2·B1", "task": "water",
   "why": "street level is drying"}
]}

What the wall said

#1 ACCEPTED · D1 → L7·B12, watering. Round trip uses about 36% battery.

#2 UNREACHABLE · D2 has 12% battery and would run flat on the way home from L2·B1.

Every refusal has a code, a plain reason, and a mark on the wall.

  • UNREACHABLE
  • JAM
  • BUSY
  • OVERWATER
  • OVERFEED
  • NOTHING TO DO
  • DEAD
  • EMPTY
  • DOCK FULL
  • OUT OF RANGE
  • UNREADABLE

Bring your own Claude API key for one-click suggestions, or paste the prompt into any chatbot and paste its reply back. Either way, the same checks apply.

04 · Measures

What the trace records

Every season keeps a running account of what the fleet did and what the wall became. The whole trace, with the proposal log, copies out as JSON.

Season score
Average share of healthy modules over the 14 days. The target is 75%.
What survived
Healthy, dead and revived modules, and a healthy-over-time chart.
Jobs done
Waterings, feedings, pest treatments and revivals.
Throughput
Jobs per game day across the fleet.
Coverage
Share of the wall tended in the last 24 game hours.
Proposals
Accepted and refused, with refusals counted by reason.
Resources
Litres of water, kilograms of fertilizer, kilowatt-hours of charge.
Live readout
Wilting, pests, decaying and hungry modules, wind at the parapet, each drone's battery and payload.
