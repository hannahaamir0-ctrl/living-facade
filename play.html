Living Facade

A 30 m south facade carries 140 planted modules. Sun, wind and pests dry and damage them a little every hour. A fleet of drones keeps them alive using local rules only: each drone sees just the modules within its sensing reach, takes the most urgent one nobody else has claimed, and flies back to the roof dock when its battery, water tank or fertilizer runs low. Tune the rules, then keep the wall at least 75% healthy across a 14-day season.

    Proposal format

    A proposal is JSON with one to three moves. Modules are named by level and bay: L1 is the street level, L10 the top; B1 is the left bay, B14 the right. Drones are D1 to D10.

    {"moves": [
  {"type": "dispatch", "drone": "D3", "module": "L7·B4",
   "task": "water | feed | treat | revive", "why": "…"},
  {"type": "set", "param": "fleet | trigger | reach",
   "value": 8, "why": "…"},
  {"type": "flag", "module": "L2·B11", "why": "…"},
  {"type": "wait", "why": "…"}
]}

    A dispatched drone drops what it was doing, claims the module and flies there on the rails. Its route is drawn in blue with the proposal number.

    Why a move is refused

    UNREACHABLE
    Not enough battery for the trip, the work and the flight home. The route is drawn with an X where the battery runs out.
    JAM
    Another drone has already claimed that module. Both drones are circled.
    BUSY
    The drone is in the middle of a job.
    OVERWATER
    Soil is above 90% moisture. More water would rot the roots.
    OVERFEED
    Nutrients are above 80%. More fertilizer would burn the plant.
    NOTHING TO DO
    No pests to treat, or nothing decaying to revive.
    DEAD
    The module is dead. Only revive works on it.
    EMPTY
    The drone's water tank or fertilizer hopper is too low for the job.
    DOCK FULL
    The roof has 10 pads. A bigger fleet has nowhere to land.
    OUT OF RANGE
    A setting outside what the controls allow.
    UNREADABLE
    The reply could not be read as a proposal, or names a drone, module or task that does not exist.

    How a drone decides

    1. Scan every module within sensing reach, plus any module you flagged.
    2. Skip modules another drone has already claimed.
    3. Score the rest: decaying and dead modules need reviving, pests need treating, dry soil needs water, hungry soil needs fertilizer. Closer modules score higher.
    4. Check the battery can cover the flight there, the work, and the flight home. If not, return to the dock.
    5. Claim the best module and fly there along the wall's joints: across on the slab lines between levels, up and down on the bay lines between modules.
    6. Give way to drones that are working or docked, and to lower-numbered drones: hold position, step aside if one needs to pass, and take a different rail if the way stays blocked.
    7. Do the work, then scan again from where you are.
    8. If nothing is in view, fly home. After an hour on the dock, fly a scouting run to a random spot.

    The wall

    Ten levels of 3 m, fourteen bays of 1.5 m. Sedum mats sit on the exposed upper levels, sedge grasses in the middle, shade ferns near the street, with climbing ivy scattered throughout. Each species dries out at its own rate and wilts at its own moisture threshold.

    A neighbouring tower shades the lower-left of the wall until late morning. Wind is stronger near the parapet, which slows drones and costs them battery. Pests spread to adjacent modules if left alone.

    No drone sees the whole wall unless you give it the reach. The pattern of care that emerges comes from these few rules.
